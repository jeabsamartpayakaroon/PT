The story

The Godfather of Muay Thai

Known across Melbourne's martial arts community as "the Godfather of Muay Thai" — and to his students as "Muay Thai Yoda" — Kru Jeab Samart Payakaroon has spent a lifetime in the sport. He started fighting at age 9 and went on to a professional career of more than 100 fights, the kind of record that very few coaches in Australia can put behind their teaching.

In 2002 he opened Sityodtong Muay Thai in Brunswick — holding formal permission to represent the legendary Sityodtong gym of Pattaya, Thailand, here in Melbourne — and has spent the two decades since producing champions and mentoring hundreds of fighters through every stage of the sport, from a first pad session to fight night. He remains active around the sport as a judge and referee at major promotions, and now coaches as Senior Coach at The Ring Gym in Braybrook.

"Muay Thai has always been my life. I love sharing what I know and helping others grow. It's a proud part of my culture and a fair, honest sport." Kru Jeab Samart Payakaroon

Students who trained with him in the Brunswick years describe a gym run the traditional Thai way: no set class structure, just open floor time where Jeab called fighters over for rounds on the pads and corrected technique on the spot. The rest was learned by watching — his coaching adapted to each fighter's individual strengths and weaknesses rather than one style for everyone. That same personalised, pads-on approach is what carries over into the private and small-group sessions on this page.

This page is for the training he runs outside of regular class hours: one-on-one and small-group sessions built around fight preparation and levelling up technique, for anyone from competing amateurs to students who simply want to train properly.

Off the mats When he's not coaching, Kru Jeab is usually at the track — a lifelong greyhound racing fan — spending time with his dogs, or watching Liverpool play.

Who this is for

Fight training, not just fitness

Fighters in camp

Pad work, sparring strategy and fight-specific conditioning for anyone with a bout on the calendar — tailored to your opponent and your gaps, not a generic class plan.

Levelling up technique

For students who've got the basics and want the details fixed — clinch, teeps, timing, defence — the small corrections that separate good from fight-ready.

Small squads

Train fight-focused sessions with a training partner or a few teammates. Same coaching, split cost, and the added benefit of live sparring reps.

Coming back / starting late

One-on-one time to build fundamentals properly and safely, at your pace, before stepping into group classes.
